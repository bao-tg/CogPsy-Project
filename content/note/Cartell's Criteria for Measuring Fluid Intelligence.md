# Cattell’s Criteria for Measuring Fluid Intelligence (1963)

## Overview
**Raymond B. Cattell (1963)** distinguished **fluid intelligence (Gf)** from **crystallized intelligence (Gc)** and proposed **explicit criteria** for how fluid intelligence should be **measured**.

Fluid intelligence refers to the capacity to **reason, detect relationships, and solve novel problems** independent of acquired knowledge.

---

## Core Criteria (Key Result)
Cattell argued that a valid measure of **fluid intelligence** should:

1. **Minimize Cultural and Educational Influence**
   - Tasks should rely as little as possible on language, schooling, or specific knowledge
   - Performance should not depend on prior learning

2. **Emphasize Novel Problem Solving**
   - Problems must be unfamiliar
   - Solutions cannot be retrieved from memory

3. **Require Abstract Reasoning**
   - Focus on identifying relations, patterns, and rules
   - Central operations include induction and deduction

4. **Limit Dependence on Learned Skills**
   - Avoid arithmetic facts, vocabulary, or factual recall
   - Use figural or symbolic material instead of verbal content

5. **Reflect Biological and Developmental Factors**
   - Sensitive to neurological integrity and aging
   - Expected to peak in early adulthood and decline with age

---

## Typical Task Characteristics
According to Cattell, suitable Gf measures often include:
- **Figural analogies**
- **Series completion**
- **Matrix reasoning**
- **Classification of abstract shapes**

These tasks aim to capture **pure reasoning capacity** rather than accumulated knowledge.

---

## Relation to the Gf–Gc Theory
- Gf and Gc are **distinct but correlated** abilities
- Gf supports the acquisition of Gc over time
- Measurement criteria ensure that Gf is not contaminated by Gc

---

## Implications
- Influenced the design of:
  - Culture-reduced intelligence tests
  - Nonverbal reasoning measures
- Provided a theoretical basis for modern tests of fluid reasoning

---

## Limitations
- Complete cultural neutrality is unattainable
- Even nonverbal tests may reflect:
  - Test-taking familiarity
  - Educational exposure

Nonetheless, Cattell’s criteria remain foundational.

---

## Historical Significance
- Formalized methodological standards for measuring fluid intelligence
- Shaped psychometric test construction for decades
- Central to contemporary intelligence theory
