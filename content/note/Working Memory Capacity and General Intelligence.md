# Working Memory Capacity and General Intelligence (Kyllonen, 1996)

## Overview
In a seminal paper, **Patrick C. Kyllonen (1996)** argued that **working memory capacity (WMC)** plays a **central role in general intelligence (g)** and in performance on **complex cognitive tasks**, especially **reasoning and problem solving**.

This work contributed to the view that **individual differences in intelligence are largely differences in working memory capacity**.

---

## Core Claim (Key Result)
Kyllonen proposed that:
> **Reasoning ability and working memory capacity are extremely closely related**, possibly reflecting the same underlying cognitive construct.

Key results:
- Measures of WMC show **very high correlations** with:
  - Fluid intelligence
  - Abstract reasoning
  - Complex problem solving
- In many analyses, **working memory tasks predict reasoning performance as well as, or better than, traditional IQ subtests**

---

## Conceptual Basis
### Working Memory Capacity (WMC)
- Refers to the ability to **maintain and manipulate information simultaneously**
- Not just short-term storage, but **controlled attention under cognitive load**
- Especially critical when:
  - Distractions are present
  - Multiple relations must be processed

---

### Relation to General Intelligence (g)
- General intelligence reflects performance across diverse cognitive domains
- Kyllonen argued that:
  - Many intelligence tests place heavy demands on **working memory**
  - Thus, **WMC may underlie g**, rather than being just one component of it

---

## Empirical Evidence
Kyllonen analyzed data from multiple cognitive tasks, including:
- **Complex span tasks** (e.g., reading span, operation span)
- **Reasoning tasks** (e.g., matrix reasoning, logical inference)

Findings:
- Correlations between WMC and reasoning often exceeded **r ≈ 0.80**
- Structural models showed that a **single latent factor** could explain both WMC and reasoning performance

---

## Implications
- Intelligence differences may stem from **capacity limits in attention and working memory**
- Explains why:
  - Reasoning breaks down under high cognitive load
  - Training that improves task-specific skills does not necessarily increase g
- Influential in later theories emphasizing **executive attention** (e.g., Engle et al.)

---

## Limitations and Debate
- High correlations do not prove **identity** between WMC and g
- Later work suggests:
  - WMC is a **major contributor** to g, but not the only factor
  - Processing speed, long-term memory, and strategy also play roles

---

## Historical Significance
- Strengthened the link between **cognitive architecture** and intelligence
- Shifted intelligence research toward **process-based explanations**
- Influenced modern models of executive control and reasoning

---
