# Retroactive Interference

## Overview
**Retroactive interference** is a phenomenon in memory research in which **newly learned information interferes with the retrieval of previously learned information**.

It is one of the two classic forms of interference-based forgetting, alongside **proactive interference**.

---

## Core Idea (Key Result)
The key finding is:
> **Learning new material can impair memory for earlier material**, especially when the two sets of information are similar.

Thus:
- **Old memories are disrupted by new learning**
- Forgetting is not solely due to decay, but also to **competition during retrieval**

---

## Experimental Basis

### Paired-Associate Learning Paradigm
A classic method for studying retroactive interference:

1. Participants learn a first list of pairs (A–B)
2. They then learn a second list:
   - **A–C** (same cues, different responses) → strong interference
   - **D–E** (different cues) → minimal interference
3. Memory for the original A–B pairs is tested

Findings:
- Recall of A–B pairs is worse after learning A–C pairs
- Interference increases with **similarity** between lists

---

## Determinants of Retroactive Interference
1. **Similarity**
   - Greater overlap in meaning or structure → more interference

2. **Amount of New Learning**
   - More interpolated material leads to greater disruption

3. **Retention Interval**
   - Effects are strongest when new learning occurs soon after original learning

---

## Theoretical Interpretation
- Retroactive interference reflects **retrieval competition**
- New memories do not erase old ones but **block access** to them
- Supports cue-dependent theories of forgetting

---

## Relation to Other Memory Concepts
- **Proactive interference**: old information interferes with new learning
- **Consolidation**: interference is stronger before memories are fully stabilized
- **Context effects**: changing context can reduce interference

---

## Applications
- Explains forgetting in:
  - Language learning
  - Studying similar subjects back-to-back
- Informs:
  - Spaced practice strategies
  - Curriculum design

---

## Limitations
- Interference effects vary across individuals and materials
- Not all forgetting can be explained by interference alone

---

## Historical Significance
- Key evidence against pure decay theories of forgetting
- Central to interference-based models of memory
