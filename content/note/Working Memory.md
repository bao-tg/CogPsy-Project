# Baddeley and Hitch’s Model of Working Memory (1974)

## Overview
The **Working Memory Model**, proposed by **Alan Baddeley and Graham Hitch (1974)**, reconceptualized short-term memory as a **multicomponent system** responsible for the temporary storage **and manipulation** of information.

This model replaced the idea of a **unitary short-term memory store** found in earlier models (e.g., Atkinson–Shiffrin).

---

## Core Idea (Key Result)
Baddeley and Hitch proposed that:
> **Working memory consists of multiple specialized subsystems**, coordinated by a central control mechanism, allowing humans to perform complex cognitive tasks such as reasoning, comprehension, and learning.

Key consequences:
- Short-term memory is **not a single, limited-capacity store**
- Different types of information can be processed **in parallel**
- Cognitive tasks do not interfere equally with one another

---

## Original Components of the Model (1974)

### 1. Central Executive
- A **domain-general control system**
- Responsible for:
  - Attentional control
  - Task switching
  - Coordination of subsystems
- **No storage capacity**
- Limited processing resources

---

### 2. Phonological Loop
Specialized for **verbal and auditory information**.

Consists of:
- **Phonological Store**
  - Holds sound-based information briefly (~2 seconds)
- **Articulatory Rehearsal Process**
  - Refreshes information via subvocal repetition

Explains effects such as:
- Phonological similarity effect
- Word length effect

---

### 3. Visuospatial Sketchpad
Specialized for **visual and spatial information**.

Functions include:
- Visual imagery
- Spatial manipulation
- Mental rotation

Relatively independent from the phonological loop.

---

## Experimental Evidence
### Dual-Task Studies
Baddeley and Hitch showed that:
- Participants could perform reasoning tasks **while holding digit sequences**
- Performance declined gradually rather than catastrophically
- Indicates **separate processing resources**, not a single short-term store

---

## What the Model Explained Better Than Earlier Models
- Why verbal and visual tasks interfere minimally
- Why STM tasks can occur alongside reasoning
- Why different forms of rehearsal exist

---

## Later Extension (Not in 1974)
In **2000**, Baddeley added the **episodic buffer**, which:
- Integrates information across subsystems and long-term memory
- Uses multidimensional representations
*(Included here for historical continuity; not part of the original 1974 model.)*

---

## Limitations
- Central executive is **underspecified**
- Neural implementation not clearly defined
- Some interactions between subsystems are more complex than initially proposed

---

## Historical Significance
- One of the **most influential models in cognitive psychology**
- Foundation for modern research in:
  - Executive function
  - Language processing
  - Neuropsychological assessment
