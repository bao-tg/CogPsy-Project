## **Visuospatial Sketchpad**

- A subsystem of **working memory** (Baddeley & Hitch, 1974).
- It **stores and manipulates visual and spatial information** — like when you imagine rotating an object in your mind or navigate a map.
- It has two main parts:
    - **Visual cache** → stores shape, color, and appearance.
    - **Inner scribe** → deals with **spatial movement** and rehearsal.
> They have limited capacity, but their capacities are largely independent

## **Shepard & Metzler (1971): Mental Rotation Experiment**

### **Aim**

To test how people manipulate **visual images in the mind** — i.e., whether we can _mentally rotate_ objects and if this takes measurable time.
### **Method**
- Participants saw **two 3D block figures** at different rotation angles.
- They had to decide if the figures were **identical** or **mirror images**.
- Researchers measured **reaction time** for the decision.
### **Findings**

- Reaction time **increased linearly** with the **degree of rotation**.  
    → e.g., objects rotated 180° took longer than those rotated 45°.
- This showed people **mentally rotate** the images, almost like physically turning them.
### **Conclusion**

Supports the **visuospatial sketchpad**:
- The mind uses a **spatial, image-based process** (not just words or logic).
- Visual and spatial information is **actively manipulated**, not passively stored.

# Experiment (Quinn & McConnell, 1996)

Participants had to **learn lists of words** under different conditions:
1. **Visual imagery condition** — they imagined a picture representing each word (e.g., for “apple,” imagine an apple).
2. **Verbal rehearsal condition** — they repeated the word silently.

While they did this, they were given **two kinds of interference tasks**:
- **Visual interference** (e.g., looking at moving patterns on a screen)
- **Verbal interference** (e.g., repeating an irrelevant sound like “the, the, the…”)
### 📊 Findings:

- **Visual interference** disrupted the **imagery-based learning** but **not** the **verbal rehearsal.**
- **Verbal interference** disrupted **verbal rehearsal** but **not** **imagery-based learning.**
### 📚 Interpretation:

This provides **strong evidence for separate subsystems** in working memory
- A **phonological loop** (for verbal info)
- A **visuospatial sketchpad** (for visual & spatial info)

It also supports the idea that **mental imagery (mind's eye) uses the same resources as visual perception (process of interpreting light signal from your eyes)**, since both are impaired by visual interference.
