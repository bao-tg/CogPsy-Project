# What is working memory?

+ A limited-capacity system for temporary storage and manipulation of information for complex tasks such as comprehension, learning, and reasoning
+ information is represented as speech, visual imagery, spatial imagination
+ buffer, rehearsal, inner voice, central excutive decide what information is rehearsed, and is forgetten

# Episodic buffer

### **Episodic Buffer (Baddeley, 2000)**

The **episodic buffer** was added later to the working memory model to solve a problem:
> How do we integrate **visual**, **verbal**, and **long-term memory** information into a single, unified experience?

### **What It Does**

- It’s a **temporary storage system** that combines (or _binds_) information from different sources:
    - phonological loop (sounds, words)
    - visuospatial sketchpad (images, locations)
    - long-term memory (past experiences)
- The result is a **multidimensional “episode”** — a short-lived mental scene or event that you can consciously experience and manipulate.

### **Key Features**

1. **Passive storage:**
    It doesn’t _process_ information itself — it just holds integrated chunks, like a mental snapshot.
2. **Multidimensional binding:**
    It can link together words, images, and meanings into one coherent unit.
3. **Interface to consciousness:**
    It’s what allows us to _be aware_ of information from the phonological loop and visuospatial sketchpad — basically, the “workspace” we can _mentally see_.
### **Example**

Imagine you’re recalling a memory:
> You remember your friend **laughing (sound)** while sitting at a **café (visual scene)** during your **birthday (context from long-term memory)**.

That whole “scene” exists in your **episodic buffer** — a temporary, integrated mental episode you can consciously picture and think about.
