The **central executive** is the **control system** in Baddeley and Hitch’s working memory model.  
It doesn’t store information itself — instead, it **monitors and manipulates** the two storage systems
- the **phonological loop** (for verbal/auditory info), and
- the **visuospatial sketchpad** (for visual/spatial info).
You can think of it like the _“supervisor”_ that makes sure each part of working memory is doing the right thing.

### Its Main Functions

1. **Updating short-term memory buffers**  
    → It constantly keeps track of what information is currently relevant, and replaces old info with new info as we think or learn.  
    (e.g., while doing mental arithmetic, updating the numbers as you add/subtract.)
2. **Setting goals and planning**
    → It helps organize thoughts toward an objective — deciding what steps to take next, like planning how to solve a problem or structure an essay.

3. **Inhibition of automatic responses**
    → It prevents impulsive or habitual actions that are not appropriate to the task.
    **(e.g., stopping yourself from reading a word when you’re asked to name its ink color in the Stroop task.)**
4. **Switching between tasks**
    → It lets you shift attention or strategies when the situation changes.  
    (e.g., switching from writing notes to checking a diagram.)
